\begin{center}
{\large\bfseries Moment-reproduction metric}\\[2pt]
{\small \texttt{moment\_reproduction.py}, \texttt{compute\_moment\_reproduction} (2026-11-02)}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Setting.}
Each family (a potential: $L_6$, \texttt{Scalar\_*}, Scat$k$, \dots) gives per-signal statistics $\phi_k(x)$.
\emph{Imposed} families are the run's own, with its saved fit; \emph{not-imposed} ones (\texttt{EXTRA}) are fitted
once on the data. Data: the run's training set $\{x_i\}_{i=1}^{n}$; model: the final samples
$\{\hat x_j\}_{j=1}^{n_s}$ of the run's smallest seed. For each $k$, let $m_k, s_k^2$ (resp.\ $\hat m_k,\hat s_k^2$)
be the sample mean and unbiased variance of $\phi_k$ on the data (resp.\ on the samples). Statistics with
$s_k^2\le10^{-12}\times$ the family's median $s^2$ are excluded.

\paragraph{Offset and noise} (in units of $s_k$):
\[
\delta_k=\frac{|\hat m_k-m_k|}{s_k},
\qquad
\nu_k=\frac{1}{s_k}\sqrt{\frac{s_k^2}{n}+\frac{\hat s_k^2}{n_s}} .
\]
$s_k\nu_k$ is the (unpooled, Welch) standard error of $\hat m_k-m_k$ for two independent samples, so
$\delta_k/\nu_k$ is the two-sample $z$-statistic; if data and model have the same mean of $\phi_k$, it is
approximately $|\mathcal N(0,1)|$ for large $n,n_s$ (central limit theorem).

\paragraph{Verdict} ($K=2$, $\rho=0.05$):
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{miss} & \delta_k> K\nu_k,\\[2pt]
\text{matched} & \delta_k\le K\nu_k\ \text{ and }\ K\nu_k<\rho,\\[2pt]
\text{can't tell} & \delta_k\le K\nu_k\ \text{ and }\ K\nu_k\ge\rho .
\end{array}
\]
The first condition is a two-sided test at level $\approx5\%$; the second requires that the test could have detected
an offset of $\rho$ data standard deviations.
Misses are further classed by $\delta_k$: tiny $<0.05\le$ small $<0.2\le$ clear $<0.5\le$ big (convention).
The summary gives, per (model, family), the fraction of statistics in each class.

\paragraph{Relative error} (table only): $(\hat m_k-m_k)/|m_k|$, shown only where $|m_k|\ge0.5\,s_k$.

\paragraph{Caveats.}
(i) In-sample: an imposed statistic tests the sampler (the SDE corrector), only not-imposed families test the
model. (ii) Independence is assumed: the 3 windows of a trajectory are correlated, so $\nu_k$ is somewhat too small.
(iii) One seed: seed-to-seed variation of the fitted model is not in $\nu_k$. (iv) Statistics are tested one by
one: for a perfect model about $5\%$ of them are misses by chance. (v) For heavy-tailed $\phi_k$ the normal
approximation of the $z$-statistic is slow to set in.
