\section*{Summary}
The goal was to redo the turbulence MGD runs with more particles and to choose the
regularisation strength $\lambda$ of the time-regularised estimator $\Theta_{\rm reg}(t)$.
Looking into how $\lambda$ acts led to a chain of findings: the block averaging used so far
(\code{n\_subsample}) did most of the smoothing, the old dense solver carried a hidden bias,
and the statistic basis turned out to be exactly rank-deficient. The solver was replaced, $\lambda$ can
now be chosen \emph{after} a run without re-simulating, and several numerical failure modes
were fixed. The most important open problem is scientific: in all turbulence runs, including
the July reference batch, \textbf{the moments are matched to $\sim10^{-3}$ for $t<0.95$ but
not near $t=1$}. The runs launched on 24 September (Section~\ref{sec:results}) show that the
moment error \emph{jumps} near the end, at a point set by the step size relative to the time
left, $h/(1-t)$, and that the samples are then physically wrong (too much small-scale energy,
no intermittency). Only schedule exponent 3 with ridge $10^{-2}$ avoided it, and it still misses
the 1\% target. On 25 September two new time grids were added (a fixed two-phase grid and an
adaptive one), each SDE step was made about a third cheaper (expected, not yet measured), and runs
testing the new grids were launched (Section~\ref{sec:launch25}).
No final $\lambda$, time grid or potential set is reported here.

Throughout, \meas\ marks something computed on data or in a controlled test, \hyp\ marks
an interpretation not yet tested directly.

\section{Starting point: the July reference batch}
51 seeds (300--350) of the full potential set (\code{L\_6}, \code{L\_6\_psi},
\code{L\_2\_lowpass}, \code{Scalar\_psi\_gaussianK}, \code{Scalar\_morlet\_gaussianK},
fourth-order \code{Mod2} scattering, real and imaginary, $Q=1$), with $M=256$ (1024-sample
windows coarse-grained twice), $J=8$, $Q=3$, $\sigma=3.5$, $n_t=40\,000$, $n_1=5000$
particles, $\lambda=5\cdot10^{-7}$, \code{n\_subsample}$\,=200$, per-step ridge
(\code{regularization}) $=10^{-2}$. SDE time: 10.6--10.7\,h per seed on H100 \meas.

\section{How $\lambda$ and \code{n\_subsample} act on $\Theta_{\rm reg}$}
The regularised problem (the time coupling follows Guth et al.'s dual score matching)
is, in the continuum limit,
\[
  M(t)\,\Theta - b(t) \;=\; \lambda\,\frac{d}{dt}\big(G(t)\,\dot\Theta - c(t)\big),
\]
with $M=\mathbb E[\nabla\phi\nabla\phi^\top]$ and $G=\mathbb E[\phi\phi^\top]$ on the walkers.
The grid spacing does not appear: $\lambda$ alone sets a smoothing length
$\ell\sim\sqrt{\lambda G/M}$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{n\_subsample} is \emph{not} part of Guth's method. It was introduced to keep
        the dense $(n r)^2$ solve in memory, and it averages $M,G,b,c$ over blocks of
        $n_s$ steps. The block averaging makes it a second smoother (a boxcar of width
        $\Delta=n_s/n_t$). The effective window is $\approx\max(\Delta,4\ell)$.
  \item In the July runs, $\lambda=5\cdot10^{-7}$ was essentially \emph{inactive} for $t<0.95$:
        $\Theta_{\rm reg}$ equals the block average of the per-step $\theta_t$ to within
        0.15--0.37 block standard errors; $\lambda$ only acts for $t>0.95$, where the Cos
        schedule makes the steps small (the coupling is $\lambda/dt^2$) \meas\ (4 seeds).
  \item The per-step $\theta_t$ noise is white (lag-1 autocorrelation of differences
        $-0.455$, vs.\ $-0.5$ for white noise), so averaging does reduce variance as expected \meas.
  \item Each block was labelled with its \emph{first} time although it holds the block
        average: a half-block time shift \meas\ (toy with known $\theta$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Solver: from dense float32 to block-Thomas float64}
\paragraph{Problems of the old dense solve} \meas\ (tests with the actual solver code)
\begin{itemize}
  \item The ridge $0.01\cdot I$ was added \emph{after} Jacobi rescaling, i.e.\ $0.01\,\mathrm{diag}(A)$
        in original units, which contains the coupling $\lambda/dt^2$. For $\ell/\Delta\gtrsim2$
        it shrinks $\Theta$ towards zero (toy with a known $\theta$ of RMS 0.87: RMS error 0.82
        at $\lambda=5\cdot10^{-5}$, $n_s=10$, vs.\ 0.004 without this ridge). So smaller \code{n\_subsample} was \emph{not} better at fixed $\lambda$.
  \item It ran in float32; on ill-conditioned systems with large $\lambda$ the error reached
        640\% (the block-Thomas solver in float32 failed similarly).
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{New solver} Block-Thomas elimination, float64, on CPU, $O(n r^2)$ memory,
no ridge inside the elimination. The system is symmetric positive definite, so no pivoting across
blocks is needed. Matches the exact solution to $10^{-14}$--$10^{-8}$ even at condition
number $10^9$ \meas. With \code{n\_subsample}$\,=1$ it solves Guth's system on the full
grid (no block averaging). On the Gaussian test case $\theta$ is recovered correctly
(checked on the cluster). Selected with \code{--reg\_solver thomas}; the old path is unchanged.

\section{Choosing $\lambda$ after the run}
$\lambda$ does not enter the SDE, only the final solve. Runs can therefore save the assembled
system ($t$, $M_k$, $G_k$, $b_k$, $c_k$; $\sim$25\,GB per run) and re-solve it offline for
any $\lambda$ (\code{--save\_reg\_system}, \code{codes/resolve\_theta\_reg.py}). The offline
solve reproduces the in-run one exactly (difference 0.0) \meas. On the cluster: 136\,s per
$\lambda$ for $r=235$, $n=39\,716$ nodes \meas.

\paragraph{Selection rule} (\code{turbulence/lamtune\_select.ipynb}) Residual
$R=\Theta_{\rm reg}(\lambda)-\Theta_{\rm reg}(0)$. While $\lambda$ only removes noise, block
means of $R$ shrink like noise and $\mathbb E[z^2]\lesssim1$; when it removes signal,
$\mathbb E[z^2]\gg1$. Choose the largest $\lambda$ with $\mathbb E[z^2]\le1$ in every time
window. On synthetic data with known $\theta(t)$ this threshold landed on the true
RMS-error optimum; a threshold of 2 overshot by half a decade (1.6$\times$ the optimal error)
\meas. The reference is $\lambda=0$ of the same solver, not $\theta_t$, whose per-step ridge
would appear as spurious structure.

\section{Numerical issues found on the turbulence runs}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{GPU memory.} The order-4 scattering gradient holds $\sim6$ complex tensors of
        shape $(B,8,8,9,256)$ at once: $\sim6.6$\,MB per sample \meas. Batches of 5000 fit
        an 80\,GB H100; a 16\,GB V100 needs $\le1000$.
  \item \textbf{Throughput.} $\approx1.9\cdot10^{-4}$\,s per particle per step on H100,
        consistent over three batches \meas. Hence $n_1^{\max}\approx 18\,\mathrm h/(n_t\cdot1.9\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm s)$:
        $\sim8500$ at $n_t=40\,000$; $n_1=10\,000$ needs $\sim21$\,h, above the 20\,h limit.
        (Updated 25 September: about a third of each step was duplicated work, now removed;
        see Section~\ref{sec:cost}.)
  \item \textbf{Dead potentials.} Two of five seeds ($n_1=10^4$, $n_t=33\,000$) crashed at
        step 32\,583 (i.e.\ $t\to1$) with a ``singular'' per-step Gram. With the ridge added after
        rescaling, a finite matrix cannot be singular, so the cause is a potential whose gradient is exactly
        zero on all walkers (0/0 in the rescaling) \meas\ (no NaN before the crash). Fix: such a
        coefficient is set to 0 for that step only; healthy steps are bit-identical to before.
        Most likely an emptied region of a \code{Scalar\_*\_gaussianK} statistic \hyp.
  \item \textbf{Rank-deficient statistic basis.} With no ridge at all, every offline solve was
        singular. The scaled $M_k$ has 2--3 eigenvalues at $10^{-16}$--$10^{-13}$ \meas. Traced to
        \code{L\_6\_psi}[20--23]: at $M=256$, $J=8$, $Q=3$ the wavelet channels 21, 22, 23 are
        \emph{exact copies} (all the single frequency bin 1), channel 20 is a near-copy (a
        $1.1\cdot10^{-4}$ component at bin 2) \meas. Removing exact copies loses nothing
        ($\theta_a\phi+\theta_b\phi=(\theta_a+\theta_b)\phi$); \code{--deduplicate\_filters} drops them
        for the channel-list potentials only (25$\to$23 channels). The offline solve uses a ridge
        $10^{-6}\,\mathrm{diag}(M_k)$ on the data term. The same happens for the Gaussian bank ($M=128$, $J=7$).
  \item \textbf{Float32 per-step solves.} With near-duplicate statistics, float32 is already
        $5\cdot10^{-4}$ off at ridge $10^{-4}$ and 66\% off at $10^{-8}$; float64 stays at
        $\sim2\cdot10^{-8}$ \meas. New option \code{--solve\_float64}.
  \item \textbf{The basis depends on $n_1$.} The \code{Scalar\_*\_gaussianK} fit keeps a
        data-dependent number of region statistics: $r=235$ at $n_1=8500$ vs.\ $r=291$ at
        $n_1=10^4$ \meas. $\theta$ is therefore not comparable coefficient-by-coefficient across $n_1$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Interpretation (as of 24 September; see Section~\ref{sec:results} for what the runs showed)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Not a particle-count problem: more particles improve the empirical moments; the run
        with \emph{more} particles but \emph{fewer} steps is the worse one \hyp.
        (25 September: $n_1$ is still the one setting that differs between the $n_1=2000$ runs,
        where ridge $10^{-4}$ worked, and the $n_1=8500$ runs, where it failed; open.)
  \item \textbf{Suspect 1: the per-step ridge.} $10^{-2}$ only partly corrects directions in
        which $G$ is nearly degenerate, and $G$ becomes more degenerate near $t=1$ (duplicate
        pairs 3$\to$6); the targets also move fastest there, so partial corrections fall behind \hyp.
        (25 September: at full size the \emph{larger} ridge is the one that helps, and only
        with exponent 3.)
  \item \textbf{Suspect 2: time resolution near $t=1$.} Consistent with $n_t=33\,000$ being
        worse than $40\,000$ \hyp. (25 September: supported by the runs, in the form of
        $h/(1-t)$. The earlier remark that a larger \code{schedule\_exponent} is free was wrong:
        the time grid is float32 and its steps near $t=1$ are quantised, see Section~\ref{sec:grid}.)
  \item \textbf{Suspect 3: statistics dying near $t=1$}, which cannot be corrected at all \hyp.
        (25 September: the worst moments at $t=1$ are matched by the ridge-$10^{-2}$ run, so they
        are not uncorrectable.)
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{A better measure of moment matching.} The error on each moment's own scale,
\[ \frac{|\bar\phi_e-\bar\phi_p|}{\max_t|\bar\phi_e|}, \]
has no spikes: at the spike times it stays at 0.7--1.6$\times$ its local median \meas. With $\sim200$
moments the mean is carried by a few, so the ensemble is summarised by the median, p90 and maximum over
moments. Ridge-$10^{-2}$ run at the final step: median $2.8\cdot10^{-6}$, p90 $1.3\cdot10^{-3}$, maximum
0.72; 11 of 197 moments above 1\% (indices 37--39, 47, 48, 63, 64, 68, 70, 75, 76) \meas. So this run meets
a 1\% target on the p90 but not for every moment. Proposed target: p90 $\le1\%$ at every $t$, plus the list of
moments above 1\% at the end, named from the fitted potentials. (The adaptive grid uses the largest
$|\bar\phi_e|$ seen \emph{so far}, the only causal choice; at $t\approx0$ some targets are near zero, so its
first steps see a large error and stay at the smallest step.)

\paragraph{$\theta$ at the jump.} $\|\theta_t\|$ and $\|\theta_t\|\,h\sigma^2$ (the correction
actually applied) rise smoothly by 4--5 decades from $1-t\approx1.5\cdot10^{-2}$ in every run,
including the one that never jumps; at each jump they only step up by less than $2\times$, together
with the moment error and $dH$ \meas. At the jumps, $h/(1-t)$ from the schedule formula is
$\approx1.9\cdot10^{-3}$ (exponent 2) and $\approx1.3\cdot10^{-3}$ (exponent 3, where float32 makes some
steps up to $\sim30\%$ longer) \meas. Interpretation: the explicit step becomes unstable once
$h/(1-t)$ exceeds $\sim1.5\cdot10^{-3}$, and ridge $10^{-2}$ damps it \hyp. ($\eta_t$ is not saved,
so the predictor could not be checked directly; the absolute size of $\theta$ is meaningless since the
turbulence potentials are not normalised.)

\section{The end of the time grid (25 September)}\label{sec:grid}
\paragraph{The cut at $1-t=5\cdot10^{-5}$ loses nothing.} The run stops at the first $t$ that rounds
to $1.0000$. At that point the Cos interpolant still carries noise with weight
$\cos(\pi t/2)\approx7.9\cdot10^{-5}$; its power is 1.1\% of the data spectrum at the Nyquist
frequency and less at all other frequencies \meas. The finest scales emerge from the noise between
$1-t\approx10^{-3}$ (noise $4.5\times$ the data at Nyquist) and $2\cdot10^{-4}$ ($0.18\times$) \meas,
which is where the runs jump.

\paragraph{Float32 quantises the steps near $t=1$.} Largest error of $h$ on the float32 grid vs.\ the
exact one \meas:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
$1-t$ & exponent 2 & exponent 3 & exponent 4\\
\midrule
$[10^{-3},5\cdot10^{-4})$ & 5\% & 11\% & 16\%\\
$[5\cdot10^{-4},2\cdot10^{-4})$ & 7\% & 19\% & 33\%\\
$[2\cdot10^{-4},10^{-4})$ & 10\% & 32\% & 49\%\\
$[10^{-4},5\cdot10^{-5})$ & 13\% & 48\% & 96\%\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
No step is zero before the cut. Exponent 4 was therefore not tried: its extra resolution near $t=1$
would be largely quantisation. With exponents 2--4 at $n_t=40\,000$, $h/(1-t)$ grows towards the end
(exponent 3: $\sim3.5\cdot10^{-4}$ at $1-t=10^{-2}$, $\sim2\cdot10^{-3}$ at the cut).

\paragraph{New time grids} (\code{codes/time\_schedules.py}, option \code{--schedule}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{power} (default): the old grid, bit-identical, with the same cut \meas.
  \item \code{two\_phase}: \code{n\_bulk} uniform steps on $[0,t_{\rm switch}]$, then steps that each
        remove the same fraction $r$ of the time left, so $h/(1-t)=r$ is constant down to
        $1-t=5\cdot10^{-5}$. $r$ is fitted to the remaining steps, so \code{--nt} is still the total
        (e.g.\ $n_t=60\,000$, \code{n\_bulk}$\,=10\,000$, $t_{\rm switch}=0.9$: $r=1.5\cdot10^{-4}$). Float64.
  \item \code{adaptive}: each step size is chosen from the previous step's moment error
        ($|\bar\phi_e-\bar\phi_p|$ divided by the largest $|\bar\phi_e|$ seen so far, averaged over moments),
        before the next noise is drawn, so no step is rejected and redrawn (which would bias the SDE).
        Growth is capped at $\times1.2$ per step and $h/(1-t)$ is kept in $[10^{-5},10^{-3}]$ by default.
        \code{--nt} is the maximum number of steps; the run stops and saves at 90\% of the time limit. Float64.
\end{itemize}
Both new grids run end to end on small local tests; they have not yet been tested at full size.

\section{Cost of a step, and the entropy increment (25 September)}\label{sec:cost}
\paragraph{Profile} (\code{turbulence/launch/profile\_step.sh}, cProfile on H100, $n_1=8500$, 385 steps):
1.57\,s per step \meas. The Gram matrix was computed three times per step (0.26\,s each, $\sim50\%$),
the third time an exact repeat of the corrector's $G(y_k)$ ($\sim17\%$); the moment velocity
$\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb E[\phi(I_t)]$ twice ($\sim33\%$), the second for the entropy increment ($\sim16\%$) \meas.
Both repeats are now reused. The expected cost is $\sim1.05$\,s per step, not yet measured.

\paragraph{The entropy increment.} $dH_k=-\theta_k\cdot\dot m$ used $\phi$ on the interpolant at $t_{k+1}$
but its velocity $\dot I$ at $t_k$, unchanged since the 11 June upload with no documented reason. The
entropy rate $dS/dt=-\theta_t\cdot\dot m_t$ has both at the same time, and $\theta_k$ belongs to $t_{k+1}$;
it now uses $t_{k+1}$ for both, which is also what the next step's predictor needs.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Exact case ($\phi=x^2$, target $\mathcal N(0,0.2^2)$, $\sigma^2=0.4$, $n_1=10^5$, scalar code),
        error of the integrated $dH$, old $\to$ new: $+0.093\to+0.069$ ($n_t=100$),
        $+0.024\to+0.017$ (400), $+0.014\to+0.013$ (1600) \meas. The old version was the least accurate
        of the variants tried. The form $-\theta_k\cdot(m_{k+1}-m_k)/h$, which a concavity argument
        supports, did a little better ($+0.034$, $+0.008$, $+0.010$) but is not used: at fine steps the
        variants agree to $\sim6\cdot10^{-3}$ and the remaining $\sim10^{-2}$ comes from $\theta$ itself
        (Euler--Maruyama bias and estimation noise).
  \item Bimodal model: the integrated $dH$ moved by 5.0\% ($n_t=200$) and 0.69\% ($n_t=1000$) \meas.
        Entropy bounds from runs before and after this change are not directly comparable.
  \item Samples, $\theta$, moments and $\Theta_{\rm reg}$ are bit-identical to the previous code on a
        small A/B run \meas; $dH$ does not enter the SDE.
\end{itemize}

\section{Open questions and next steps}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Do the new time grids remove the jump at ridge $10^{-4}$, and reach 1\%
        (Section~\ref{sec:launch25})? If so, choose between \code{two\_phase} and \code{adaptive} on
        accuracy against steps. If not, check the predictor directly (save $\eta_t$) or the conditioning.
  \item Why did ridge $10^{-4}$ work at $n_1=2000$ and fail at $n_1=8500$? $n_1$ and the batch size are
        the only settings that differ.
  \item Agree on the 1\% target (proposed: own-scale p90 at every $t$, and the named list of moments
        above 1\% at the end), and find out which statistics the 11 failing moments of the ridge-$10^{-2}$
        run are (names on the cluster).
  \item $\lambda$ per potential set from the offline sweep; then choose the minimal set from
        sample quality (spectrum, structure functions, histograms, KL), which does not depend on
        $\lambda$. The reduced sets at ridge $10^{-4}$ from 24 September share the jump, so they do
        not settle this.
  \item Larger $n_1$: the data give 30\,000 series of 512 samples. Within one 20\,h job,
        steps$\times n_1\lesssim5\cdot10^8$ at the expected new step cost, so doubling $n_1$ halves
        the steps. Beyond that: checkpoint the SDE state and chain jobs. Whether the order-4 gradient
        dominates the remaining cost was not separated in the profile.
  \item Near-duplicate statistics (channel 20 vs.\ 21, some \code{Scalar\_psi} region pairs) are
        kept: they carry a little information. Only their combination is well determined, which
        matters when interpreting individual $\theta$ coefficients.
\end{enumerate}
